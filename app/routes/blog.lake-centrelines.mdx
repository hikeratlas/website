# Lake centrelines: labels that follow the lake (Nov 3, 2026)

_(Return to the [homepage](/).)_

_Disclosure: this post describes an improvement that Claude Code made, and Claude wrote the post's initial draft._

British Columbia has a lot of long, skinny, bendy lakes. The usual way to label a lake is to put its name at a single point. For a lake like that, the name ends up mostly on land:

<div className="flex justify-center items-center">
  <img src="/lake-centrelines-comparison.png"/>
</div>

Printed maps solve this by curving the name along the lake. You can do the same in MapLibre: give it a line through the middle of the lake, and label it with `symbol-placement: line`. Getting that line is the hard part.

## How to find the middle of a lake

The trick is a [Voronoi diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_diagram):

<div className="flex justify-center items-center">
  <img src="/lake-centrelines-steps.png"/>
</div>

1. Put points every 100 m along the shore.
2. Compute the Voronoi diagram of those points. Each cell is the area closer to its point than to any other.
3. Keep the cell edges that are inside the lake. They're equally far from two bits of shore, so they run down the middle. This gives a skeleton of the lake, with a dead end at every little bay and point.
4. Find the longest paths through the skeleton, from one dead end to another.
5. Of the 5 longest, take the straightest, and smooth it. That's where the label goes.

## Prior art

None of this is my idea.

- [osm-lakelines](https://github.com/lukasmartinelli/osm-lakelines), by Lukas Martinelli, built lake lines like these for [OpenMapTiles](https://openmaptiles.org/) back in 2016.
- It used [label_centerlines](https://github.com/ungarj/label_centerlines), from EOX, which implements the steps above.
- [acalcutt's fork](https://github.com/acalcutt/osm-lakelines) published planet-wide releases.

I started with acalcutt's last release, from April 2022. But lakes mapped since then didn't have a line. And it only covers lakes, so bays and straits get nothing: no label along the [Strait of Georgia](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/13321885), for example. So I wanted to regenerate the lines from current OSM data.

## Making it fast

I pointed label_centerlines at British Columbia. After 14 minutes it still hadn't finished, which seemed like a lot for one province.

So I profiled a single lake: Trout Lake, which took 45 seconds on its own. 90% of the time was in shortest-path searches.

To find the longest paths, label_centerlines runs a search between _every pair_ of dead ends in the skeleton. A skeleton has a dead end for every little bay. Trout Lake needed about 4,900 searches, and the count grows with the square of the number of dead ends, so big lakes take forever.

It turns out you only need three searches:

<div className="flex justify-center items-center">
  <img src="/lake-centrelines-search.png"/>
</div>

This is a classic trick for finding the longest path in a tree, and the skeleton is a tree, except around islands. Start at any dead end and search. The farthest dead end, a, must be one end of the longest path. Search again from a: the farthest dead end from it, b, is the other end. One more search from b gives some runner-up candidates.

Along with vectorizing some per-edge Python with Shapely 2, that took:

- Trout Lake from **45 s to 0.02 s**
- all of British Columbia from **"didn't finish in 14 minutes" to 7 s**
- all of North America to **about 3 minutes**, on one of GitHub's free 4-core runners. Pulling the water out of the PBF with osmium takes another 10 minutes.

North America now has 37,619 lines, up from 19,053 in the 2022 release.

It isn't perfect. Lakes with lots of islands, like Babine Lake, only get a line along part of their length, because the islands break the skeleton into pieces. osm-lakelines has the same problem.

## Try it

The tool is at [hikeratlas/lake-centrelines](https://github.com/hikeratlas/lake-centrelines). Give it an OSM PBF, and it gives you one GeoJSON line per named lake:

```
./lake-centrelines.sh british-columbia-latest.osm.pbf lakes.geojsonl
```

The labels are live on [the map](/map). Try the [Strait of Georgia](/map#7/49.3/-123.8).
